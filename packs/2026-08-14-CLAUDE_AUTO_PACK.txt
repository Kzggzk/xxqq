15th Five-Year Plan Set to Drive China's Deficit-to-GDP Ratio North of 4% 
China Banking News examines how the spending priorities encoded in China's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030) will structurally push the official deficit-to-GDP ratio above 4%, exceeding the informal 3% ceiling that Beijing maintained for decades. The plan prioritizes advanced manufacturing, semiconductor self-sufficiency, green energy infrastructure, and social welfare expansion, all of which require sustained central government outlays. The article argues that markets should interpret the deficit expansion as a deliberate policy signal rather than fiscal distress, with Chinese policymakers viewing higher deficits as necessary to offset weak private-sector demand.

The Macroeconomic Policy Implications of China's 2026 Two Sessions 
China Banking News analyzed the policy signals emerging from the 2026 Two Sessions (National People's Congress and Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference), which formally launched the 15th Five-Year Plan covering 2026-2030. China set a GDP growth target of approximately 5% for 2026 and endorsed a continuation of 'moderately loose monetary policy,' with economists forecasting two interest rate cuts totaling 20-30 basis points during the year. The article examines how the fiscal stance — featuring a broad deficit ratio rising from 8.4% to 8.5% of GDP — reflects Beijing's commitment to stimulus-led stabilization amid trade war headwinds.

China's Government Debt Set to Grow by a Trillion Dollars in 2026 
Analysis from China Banking News projects China's broad fiscal deficit will rise from 11.86 trillion yuan in 2025 to approximately 12.45 trillion yuan in 2026, adding roughly one trillion dollars in government debt obligations. The issuance of ultra-long-term special government bonds is expected to double from 1 trillion yuan in 2024 to approximately 2 trillion yuan in 2026, as Beijing uses off-balance-sheet instruments to fund infrastructure and strategic industrial investment. The piece flags sustainability concerns as the debt-to-GDP ratio trends higher, particularly in the context of deflationary pressure and a property sector still recovering from its multi-year downturn.
